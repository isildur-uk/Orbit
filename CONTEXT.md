# ORBIT Personal Network domain glossary

This glossary defines the neutral personal-network vocabulary. The existing
law-enforcement charting and registry products retain their own operational
terms; this context is for the personal-network sibling surface.

## Person

A real individual represented in the network. A Person may have multiple names,
handles, addresses, organisations, contact methods and source records.

## Contact Profile

The user-facing address-book view of a Person. It is a current, useful summary
assembled from known details, relationship signals, interactions, facts and
evidence. It is not a second Person record.

## Interaction

A dated exchange or encounter involving one or more People. Examples include an
email, message, call, meeting, event, note or introduction.

## Relationship

A connection between two entities, including the user's relationship to a
Person. A relationship has strength, recency, frequency, trend and evidence;
it is not limited to a single score.

## Fact

A structured statement about a Person, relationship or opportunity. Examples
include a role change, current project, interest, need, offer or commitment.
Facts can expire or be superseded.

## Evidence

The source item that supports a Fact, Interaction or calculated conclusion.
Every surfaced fact or recommendation must distinguish user-entered data,
private source data, public source data, calculated metrics and AI inference.

## Historical Context

The time-aware view of a Contact Profile: what was current at a selected date,
what changed afterwards and which evidence supports each change. Historical
Context is not a second person record and it does not rewrite older facts when
a newer fact appears.

## Vault

The user-owned, portable collection of people, interactions, facts,
opportunities, sources and attachments shared by ORBIT desktop and mobile
clients. A vault is the durable source of truth; graph layouts, scores and
summaries are derived views.

## Opportunity

An actionable candidate detected from needs, offers, expertise, introductions,
commitments, shared interests or changes in the network. An Opportunity is a
recommendation to review, not an instruction or an automatically performed
action.

## Profile sections

The Contact Profile should use these neutral sections:

- Identity and contact methods
- How we know each other
- Current context
- Work, projects and organisations
- Interests and expertise
- Needs, offers and goals
- Relationship health
- Shared contacts and network position
- Interactions and timeline
- Commitments and opportunities
- Notes and evidence

Law-enforcement-specific fields such as risk, warnings, criminality,
government classification and agency handling are not part of this profile.
